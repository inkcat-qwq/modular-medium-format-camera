# BODY2_REV05 — Architecture Integration

> **Status:** CANDIDATE / SIMULATION  
> **Development stage:** Pre-prototype  
> **Validation:** CAD and simulation only  
> **Date:** September 2026

BODY2_REV05 represents a major system-integration stage of the Modular Medium Format Camera project.

This revision brought several previously independent development paths into a single camera architecture for the first time.

It is not a manufacturing release and should not be interpreted as a finalized camera design.

---

## Objective

The purpose of this revision was to evaluate whether the major camera subsystems could coexist within a single coherent structural architecture.

The integration focused on:

- the central structural core
- the rear digital-back interface
- the direct optical viewfinder
- the side-shoulder finder structure
- the front-module interface
- serviceable electronics and grip structures
- mechanical datum and clamping concepts

The objective was not to finalize individual parts, but to identify conflicts between subsystems before physical prototyping.

---

## Structural Architecture

Earlier revisions treated several camera functions as more independent packaging problems.

BODY2_REV05 moved toward a more clearly defined central structural core that acts as the primary reference for the camera platform.

This core is intended to support:

- front optical modules
- rear digital-back adapters
- the viewfinder structure
- serviceable body components
- future electronic systems

The current body dimensions and mechanical details remain developmental candidates rather than manufacturing specifications.

---

## Viewfinder Integration

A major goal of this revision was to integrate the current direct optical viewfinder architecture into the camera body.

The finder is no longer treated simply as an external accessory.

Instead, the architecture now includes:

- a fixed structural support
- a removable optical carrier
- a serviceable outer enclosure
- dedicated front and eyepiece openings

This arrangement allows the optical assembly and the outer camera shell to perform different functions.

The structural support is responsible for alignment, while the outer enclosure primarily provides packaging and protection.

---

## Why the Direct-View Finder Was Retained

Earlier relay-based finder concepts introduced significant optical and ergonomic difficulties.

The current direct-view architecture reduces optical complexity and provides a clearer mechanical integration path.

However, the current finder remains a simulation candidate.

Important unresolved issues include:

- eye-position tolerance
- interaction with the user's face
- real digital-back clearance
- optical mounting repeatability
- physical lens packaging
- full-field validation

These questions must be tested physically before the finder architecture can be considered validated.

---

## Rear Interface Direction

The rear interface continued to evolve around the principle that:

- positioning
- axial seating
- clamping

should remain separate functions where practical.

The current preferred direction uses defined reference features to establish position and seating while allowing the clamping system to perform only the retention function.

This architecture is currently preferred because its mechanical responsibilities are easier to analyze independently.

It has not yet been proven to provide superior physical repeatability.

An alternative interface architecture is still retained for comparison.

---

## Digital-Back Integration

The digital back is currently represented as a reference subsystem rather than a completely solved interface.

The project still lacks confirmed information for several important physical details, including:

- the true seating geometry
- the locking mechanism
- the complete installation and removal path
- the exact imaging-plane relationship
- some operational clearances

No inferred or placeholder dimension should therefore be treated as a production interface specification.

---

## Front Module

BODY2_REV05 reserves space and structural interfaces for a compact front module.

The revision establishes the architectural relationship between the front module and the central core, but does not yet define a complete focusing or lens mechanism.

Future work must integrate:

- the actual lens mounting system
- focusing travel
- the imaging-axis relationship
- bellows or sealing requirements
- electrical connections where required

The front module therefore remains an architectural interface rather than a finished subsystem.

---

## Serviceability

The revision also introduced more deliberate service access.

Major components are intended to be removable without requiring the precision alignment structure of the entire camera to be disassembled.

Examples include:

- the optical finder module
- rear adapters
- grip covers
- electronics carriers
- future front modules

The detailed fasteners and service procedures remain developmental.

---

## Validation Performed

The architecture has been evaluated through:

- CAD integration
- assembly and removal studies
- interference checks
- optical-path clearance studies
- subsystem packaging analysis
- native CAD reopen verification

These checks indicate that the architecture is sufficiently coherent for continued development.

They do not demonstrate manufacturing readiness, structural durability, ergonomic suitability, or optical performance of a physical camera.

---

## Major Open Issues

The following areas remain unresolved:

- physical digital-back interface geometry
- real-world digital-back clearance
- finder ergonomics
- optical mounting and retention
- compact front-module design
- structural materials and manufacturing tolerances
- environmental sealing
- complete tool and assembly access
- frameline / HUD implementation
- physical prototype repeatability

---

## Current Conclusion

BODY2_REV05 is best treated as an **architecture-integration candidate**.

Its primary value is that the major camera subsystems can now be evaluated as parts of one system rather than as isolated design studies.

The revision should continue into targeted physical validation rather than further cosmetic refinement.

Future changes should be driven by measured mechanical, optical, and ergonomic evidence.
